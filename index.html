Each write pulse injects a kink pair into the misfit-dislocation (MD) superlattice lines. Kinks are fractonic charges — immobile topological defects of the MD-chain subsystem symmetry — so Q ∈ ℤ labels disconnected sectors of Feff. Relaxation to the normal state requires a rare kink-pair annihilation past the grain-boundary and dislocation-crossing junctions that pin the lines: τ ∝ eEtop+nxEpin.

About the model
F(ψ, n) = α(T) ψ² + ½β ψ⁴ + (λ/6) ψ⁶ + ½κ(n - np)² - g n ψ² - h(t) n + EkQ - gkQ ψ²

ψ: superconducting order parameter of the Al-Si interface condensate. n: charge density trapped in interface (misfit-dislocation) states of the Si gap.

▥ α(T) = α₀(T/Tc - 1), Tc ≈ 1.2 K for bulk Al; the metastable state lives above 2 K in the experiment.

▥ -g n ψ²: trapped charge screens the interface Coulomb repulsion and renormalizes the quadratic term, αeff = α - g n < 0 → pairing favoured. This is the trapped-charge stabilization.

▥ ½κ(n - np)²: trap capacitance. Since n follows ψ² (n* = np + gψ²/κ), eliminating n renormalizes the quartic: βeff = β - g²/κ < 0, turning the single well into a bistable landscape. A larger condensate holds more screening charge, which in turn sustains the condensate: self-stabilization by the condensate itself.

▥ -h(t) n: transient light drive during the femtosecond pulse (photoinjection into the traps). np carries a persistent fraction p (deep traps) and a slow leak τn.

▥ Topological kink sector (fracton / Frenkel–Kontorova): The Al–Si interface carries a misfit-dislocation (MD) superlattice. Each MD line is a 1-D chain on the periodic substrate potential of the interface — a Frenkel–Kontorova chain — with its own subsystem U(1) phase symmetry. Each W pulse injects kink pairs: a kink advances the winding number of its line by one, and the total kink number Q is a conserved topological charge. In fracton–elasticity duality such charges are fractonic: kinks are lineons that can only glide along their own MD line and cannot disappear without meeting an anti-kink (Pretko & Radzihovsky, PRL 120, 195301 (2018); fractonic superfluids: arXiv:2010.03261). Because Q ∈ ℤ cannot change continuously, the system cannot relax smoothly to ψ = 0: escape from the SC basin requires a rare, thermally activated kink-pair annihilation with rate γ = γ₀e−Etop — topological protection. Each kink also pins pairing through its strain field: −gkQ ψ², at a sector cost EkQ. This is a third stabilization channel, on top of trapped charge and condensate self-feedback, and it is what makes metastability a programmable memory (Yursa et al., arXiv:2607.14567).

▥ Grain-boundary pinning and dislocation crossings: The Al film is polycrystalline: each MD line terminates at grain boundaries and is crossed by forest dislocations. These junctions are pinning sites for kinks (classic Cottrell-type locking and forest hardening of dislocations). A kink glides freely along its line but must thermally depin at each junction, so annihilating a kink–antikink pair across nx junctions requires an escape barrier Eesc = Etop + nxEpin, exponentially extending the non-volatile lifetime. Pinning also slows the winding during a write pulse (τQ → τQ(1 + 0.5 nxEpin)) and the forced annihilation during an erase pulse. At extreme pinning the kink pair cannot separate within the pulse, the winding stays incomplete and the write fails — a write-fidelity trade-off: pinning protects memory, but too much of it prevents writing.

Note: the implementation here is a phenomenological integer-sector caricature — Q is treated as a single global winding number with discrete rare-event dynamics, not a full fracton tensor-gauge calculation.

▥ (λ/6)ψ⁶: saturation term bounding the landscape (strong-coupling / interface corrections).

The pulse also seeds ψ (templating by the moiré-like misfit-dislocation superlattice). A write pulse drives the system over the saddle into the SC basin; once there, trapped charge + condensate feedback keep a finite barrier ΔF against the normal state. The state can be erased (pair-breaking + de-trapping pulse) and rewritten, i.e. programmable superconductivity.
